Who Am I?

An Environmental Engineer with passion for Geophysics, driven by venturing into nature and analyzing the data it provides.

Current Research and Academic Background

I am a researcher primarily focused on Hydrology and Environmental Sciences. My experience includes field instrumentation, data collection, numerical modeling, and data analysis. Additionally, I taught several engineering courses over six years and offered Python programming courses.

Currently, I am running my Hydrological Modelling research at the University of Idaho at the Dr. Meng Zhao's group. I am conducting a study on groundwater and evapotranspiration modeling in a project with U.S. Geological Survey (USGS). This project aims to improve the plant-hydraulic modeling processes description on the influence of groundwater on evapotranspiration and ultimately in the water balance over the Contiguous United States (CONUS).

My research interests revolve around studying data-driven hydrological processes through remote-sensing, eddy-covariance flux data, and field work instrumentation.

Research

My research follows water as it moves between the soil, vegetation, groundwater, and the atmosphere, combining field measurements, remote sensing, and numerical models. These are the main themes I have been working on.

Groundwater–Vegetation Interactions

At the University of Idaho, in a project funded by the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS 104g), I coupled the MODFLOW 6 groundwater flow model to a plant hydraulics model, representing the exchange of water between the unsaturated zone and the aquifer in the Snake River Basin. The goal is to evaluate how groundwater availability controls evapotranspiration and vegetation water stress, particularly during droughts.

Related work: main paper in preparation; preliminary results presented at the AGU Fall Meetings 2024 (poster) and 2025 (oral).

Terrestrial Water Storage and Drought

The GRACE and GRACE-FO satellites have been measuring terrestrial water storage (TWS) since 2002, but this record is still too short to put current droughts and water-storage changes into a long-term perspective. Together with Dr. G. L. Harley and Dr. Meng Zhao, I am combining dendrochronological (tree-ring) records with GRACE-based TWS anomalies to investigate the potential of reconstructing TWS back in time, at the centennial scale.

I am also interested in how flash droughts develop, expand, and propagate. Using high-resolution root-zone soil moisture from CONUS404 (1980–2022), we detected flash-drought events at the pixel level and merged them into coherent three-dimensional structures in space and time across the Contiguous United States.

Related work: (Hoeltgebaum et al., 2026, preprint); flash-drought results presented at the AMS Annual Meeting 2026 (poster).

Evapotranspiration and Surface Fluxes

During my PhD at the Federal University of Paraná (UFPR), I developed the Analog Period Method (APM) to fill gaps in latent heat flux measurements from AmeriFlux sites, searching for similar periods within the dataset to reconstruct the missing values. With these continuous series, I combined flux data with hydrological measurements (discharge, precipitation, soil moisture) and remote sensing products (SMAP, MODIS, GRACE) to evaluate the storage and evapotranspiration terms of the water budget of an agricultural watershed, and to assess the lack of energy balance closure of flux towers using independent hydrological data.

More recently, I have supported the evaluation of satellite- and model-based evapotranspiration products against GRACE/GRACE-FO and in-situ observations, showing that leading products do not fully capture the interannual variability of evapotranspiration.

Related work: (Hoeltgebaum and Dias, 2021); (Hoeltgebaum, Dias, and Costa, 2023); (Zhao, Hoeltgebaum, et al., 2025).

Lakes and Reservoirs

I collaborated on STAEBLE, a simple mass-transfer lake evaporation model that uses MODIS water temperature and land-based meteorological data, calibrating the transfer coefficient through long-term energy budget closure. Tested at Lake Mead, it accounts for atmospheric stability and performs well without requiring local measurements.

During my master's, I worked on a numerical model to estimate greenhouse gas emissions from reservoirs, and applied a backpropagation artificial neural network to fill gaps in latent heat flux measured over a reservoir.

Related work: (Dias, Hoeltgebaum, and Santos, 2023); Ciência e Natura.

Experience

Natural Streamflow Reconstruction (2022–2024)

At AquaOrbis Environmental Consulting, in collaboration with LACTEC, I worked on a Brazilian national project for the National Electric System Operator (ONS) to reconstruct daily naturalized streamflow series (1931–2020) for rivers regulated by hydropower plants. I reviewed the natural streamflow series and the methods used to derive them, checked the consistency of discharge and reservoir operational data, and fitted stage–area–volume curves while assessing sediment inputs and reservoir siltation. Working closely with the operators and agencies that use these series shaped my interest in applied, stakeholder-facing research.

Field Work

I have around ten years of experience with field measurements, from river discharge measurements to the installation and maintenance of micrometeorological towers. This helps me understand the quality and the limitations of the data I use in my models.

  • Rio Verde Experimental Watershed (2016–2019): together with my colleagues and professors at the LEMMA Laboratory (UFPR), I maintained two meteorological stations, a streamflow gage, and a micrometeorological station over the Rio Verde Reservoir until the watershed's decommissioning, including discharge, water temperature, and water quality measurements. Over ten years of meteorological, hydrological, and micrometeorological data were collected at this site.
  • Amazon Rainforest (2014–2015): I assisted researchers from the GoAmazon and ATTO Tower projects in a hydrocarbon emissions campaign and a micrometeorological installation campaign — also a great opportunity to experience this incredible environmental sanctuary!
  • SIMEPAR (2014): calibration, gauging, and maintenance of hydro-meteorological sensors.
  • Itaipu Reservoir (2012–2013): installation and maintenance of a micrometeorological station on a small island in the reservoir.
